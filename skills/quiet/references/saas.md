# SaaS marketing

Quiet upgrades noisy SaaS landings into efficient, tasteful product sites. It does **not** turn them into personal portfolios.

## Job

A founder has a bad landing page. Keep the business sections: hero, product, features, social proof, pricing, FAQ, final CTA, footer. Keep recognition of the product. Raise craft.

## What tasteful SaaS does

1. **Real product UI is the hero visual.** High fidelity: sidebar, filters, issue ids, owners, tabular dates, activity. Not emoji cards, not “screenshot coming soon,” not abstract blobs.
2. **One primary CTA grammar.** One filled button style for the whole page. Secondary actions are text links (or a quiet border used sparingly), never a competing twin. On dark grounds prefer a light filled primary. On light grounds prefer a dark filled primary.
3. **Color only as meaning.** Near-monochrome page. One considered accent for brand mark or status data, never purple gradient soup or glow CTAs.
4. **Confident type, not costume.** Controlled headlines with tight tracking. Body about 14–16px. Hierarchy from weight and muted color as much as from size.
5. **Density where the product shows.** Marketing chrome can breathe. The embedded app is information-dense so a practitioner recognizes a tool.
6. **Calm pricing.** Table or quiet cards. “Recommended” in text. No “Most popular” glow badges.
7. **Specific copy.** Owners, cycles, dates, shipping. No stock phrases (supercharge, seamless, unlock the future).

## Avoid: shadcn / Vercel default grammar

These read clean but generic. Do not treat them as Quiet taste:

- Inter or Geist as the only “personality,” with cool zinc neutrals (`#111` / `#737373` / `#f7f7f7`)
- Black fill primary + outlined secondary CTA pair
- Muted gray panel hosting a bordered white component as the default exhibit
- Actions menu with keyboard hints presented as the taste centerpiece
- Component-gallery collage heroes (Vercel/Geist marketing default)
- Feature grids of identical bordered cards
- Huge negative-tracked marketing display that outshouts the product

## Anti-patterns (fail)

- Portfolio / document layouts (personal link grids, “Now” essays) as the SaaS page
- Purple/indigo gradient heroes, glowing CTAs, emoji feature cards
- Mismatched button sizes and styles
- Fake dashboards and placeholder product art
- Gray bordered card grids for every section
- Decorative annotations, rulers, corner version labels
- Em dashes in UI or marketing copy

## Procedure

1. Inventory sections and real product claims.
2. Plan: tokens, CTA grammar, which product surface is the hero fragment.
3. Wait for OK.
4. Build the product fragment first at genuine fidelity.
5. Restyle surrounding sections to support it.
6. Critique against this file, `bans.md`, and `critique-rubric.md`.

## Product fragment checklist

- Identifiable app chrome (nav or sidebar)
- Real rows or cards with owners and tabular dates
- Status as small color data, not decoration
- Enough density that a practitioner recognizes a tool
- Light/dark matches the marketing page
